What belongs in a yacht management plan

A useful management plan begins with the way you use your yacht, then gives every recurring task, decision, and update a clear place.

Las Olas / Ownership perspectives

Begin with your calendar

Before discussing a service schedule, describe the season you want to have. Note the months you expect to be aboard, the places you intend to visit, and the occasions that matter most. Include quieter periods, too. A yacht used every weekend needs a different rhythm from one awaiting a family holiday. These details give the management conversation a practical starting point and help establish priorities.

Make responsibility visible

A clear plan describes what is included, how often it is arranged, and who is responsible for coordinating it. Interior care, exterior presentation, maintenance coordination, crew arrangements, and guest preparation may all involve different people. Ask how those people connect and where requests should go. One agreed point of contact, supported by a written scope, makes it easier to follow progress without managing every conversation yourself.

Agree on the update you need

Decide what you would like to see in a regular update: completed work, upcoming visits, photographs, open decisions, or changes to the calendar. Agree which matters require your approval and how those requests will reach you. The right level of detail is personal. A concise, consistent report should let you understand the yacht's current position, ask informed questions, and prepare for your next time aboard.
