Planning a refit around your season

A well-defined project starts with your next intended use. Work backward from that date to clarify scope, dependencies, decisions, and a realistic return window.

Las Olas / Ownership perspectives

Define the result before the work

Describe what you want to be different when the project is complete. Perhaps the interior should better suit longer stays, or several planned upgrades need to happen during the same yard period. Distinguish essential work from optional additions and discuss that distinction with your project coordinator. A written scope creates a shared reference for conversations with specialist providers and helps keep later decisions connected to your original priorities.

Give decisions room in the calendar

Share your intended return date early, then ask for a schedule that identifies the decisions needed along the way. Materials, specialist availability, and the order of work can affect timing. Agree when selections and approvals are due, and discuss how changes will be handled. Leave space between the anticipated completion date and your first planned trip so the team can arrange the agreed handover and final preparations.

Plan the handover at the beginning

Agree in advance how completion will be documented and who will review the finished work with you. Depending on the project, that may include progress photographs, provider records, updated manuals, or a list of remaining items. Ask your coordinator to distinguish completed work from anything still being resolved. A clear handover gives you an organized account of the project and a useful reference for future conversations about the yacht.
